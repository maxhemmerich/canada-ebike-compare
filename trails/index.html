Where you may ride an e-bike: trails, parks and paths, one land manager at a time

Road rules are one question; the ground under the wheels is another. An e-bike can be legal in a province and still barred from the trail it is on, because the trail is the land manager's decision, not the province's. This page sets out, manager by manager, what is allowed on the trails, in each authority's own words and dated. Every figure is published by the authority named; nothing here is this site's opinion.

A permitted e-bike is non-throttled, provides up to 500 W of continuous maximum output and stops assisting when pedalling stops or 32 km/h is reached — a class 1 e-bike. Other types or classes of e-bike are not permitted on designated cycling pathways and trails in provincial parks, provincial recreation areas and wildland provincial parks.

Read the two columns against each other, because they disagree on purpose. The class numbers are the manufacturers' convention; a land manager often decides the other way round — Parks Canada and the NCC allow a pedal-assist machine that looks like a bicycle and turn away a throttle bike — while BC Parks sorts by class and lets a Class 2 or Class 3 machine onto a trail only where a motor vehicle may go. The same bike can be welcome on one manager's trail and barred from the trail beside it.

Every authority, and what it says

Each page below was read on the date shown, and the rule in the table above is taken from the quoted text. Nothing on this page comes from memory.

  • Parks Canada — national parks and national historic sites · read 2026-10-09 · Parks Canada, “Visitor guidelines — Pedal assist e-bikes”: https://parks.canada.ca/voyage-travel/regles-rules
    Pedal assist electric bicycles (e-bikes) are allowed on designated bike trails at select national parks. Contact the park you are planning to visit to find out which trails you are allowed to ride. [...] The motor can generate a maximum of 500W. Power assistance stops when the bicycle attains a speed of 32 km/h on level ground. Please note that e-bikes equipped with an accelerator (a throttle) are not pedal assist e-bikes and can only be ridden on roads.
  • Parks Canada — Banff National Park (superintendent's order) · issued 28 March 2025, read 2026-10-09 · Parks Canada, Banff National Park, “Restricted Activity: Electric bicycles (e-bikes)”: https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/ab/banff/bulletins/8942f86e-3565-4b9a-a424-7c86d7c97701
    The use of electric bicycles (e-bikes) is prohibited in Banff National Park except on paved roads and highways. Exceptions: Pedal assist e-bikes are permitted on trail segments, and networks listed below. [...] Trail segments and networks where pedal assist e-bikes are permitted [...] it has a total power output rating of 500 W or less, [...] is incapable of providing further assistance when the bicycle attains a speed of 32 km/h on level ground, [...] is equipped with a safety mechanism that prevents the motor from being engaged before the bicycle attains a speed of 3 km/h. [...] Violators may be charged under the Canada National Parks Act: maximum penalty $25 000.
  • BC Parks — provincial parks, protected areas and conservancies · read 2026-10-09 · BC Parks, “Cycling — E-bikes”: https://bcparks.ca/plan-your-trip/things-to-do/cycling/
    Electric bikes are welcome in many BC Parks. [...] Class 1 e-bikes are allowed where cycling is already permitted, unless signs indicate that a trail is closed to e-bikes. Class 2 and 3 e-bikes are usually allowed where motor vehicles are permitted, such as on roads and off-roading tracks. [...] Class 1: 32 km per hour; Class 2: 32 km per hour; Class 3: 45 km per hour.
  • Recreation Sites and Trails BC — established recreation trails on Crown land · policy dated 2019, read 2026-10-09 · Recreation Sites and Trails BC, “E-bike Policy”: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/sports-recreation-arts-and-culture/outdoor-recreation/camping-and-hiking/recreation-sites-and-trails/ebike_policy_final_04-25-2019.pdf
    Default Designations. The following default designations apply unless specific exemptions, prohibitions or allowances are provided [...] (a) All classes of e-bikes are permitted on Established Recreation Trails open to both motorized and non-motorized use, (b) Class 1 e-bikes / MAC are permitted on Established Recreation Trails open to non-motorized use unless e-bikes are specifically prohibited. (c) Class 2 and 3 e-bikes are not permitted on Established Recreation Trails that prohibit motorized vehicles.
  • National Capital Commission — Capital Pathway and Gatineau Park · read 2026-10-09 · National Capital Commission, “Share the Path”: https://ncc-ccn.gc.ca/places/share-the-path
    Rules for electric power-assisted vehicles. Here are the devices permitted and prohibited on the Capital Pathway and parkways when they are open for active use only. Permitted: Electric power-assisted bicycles (“e-bikes”), with operable pedals, that resemble conventional bicycles; Electric power-assisted cargo bicycles (“cargo e-bikes”), with operable pedals, for personal use; Motorized mobility aids, including but not limited to powered wheelchairs and scooters with three to five wheels. Prohibited: Electric power-assisted cargo bicycles for commercial use; Electric kick-style scooters; All devices that are exclusively powered by a throttle; All other electric-powered vehicles that are not specified above. [...] Travel at speeds that allow you to react in time for whatever might arise. (Maximum 20 km/h)

The electronic assist must stop when either pedaling stops or 32 km/h is reached. This is sometimes referred to as a class 1 e-bike. [...] Other types or classes of e-bikes are not permitted on designated cycling pathways and trails in provincial parks, provincial recreation areas, and wildland provincial parks.
